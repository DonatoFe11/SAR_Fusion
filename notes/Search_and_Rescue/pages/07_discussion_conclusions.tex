\chapter{Discussion and Conclusions}
\label{chap:discussion}

\section{Answers to the research questions}

\subsection{RQ1: experimental reliability}

The observed RT-DETR variability is large enough to change single-run model
rankings. Nominally identical native-CUDA executions diverge after the first
backward pass even when initialization, input, and initial loss agree. The
transferred COCO person head reduces amplification of those perturbations but
does not remove them, and DCNv2 prevents strict deterministic FAM training in
the studied environment.

Reliable comparison consequently requires more than recording a seed. The
final protocol combines paired seed values, isolated processes, a fixed
training horizon, final checkpoints, identical pretrained-head transfer,
training-only Modal Dropout, and distributional reporting. The validation
audit is equally important: a small shifted validation set can be informative
about domain mismatch while remaining unsuitable for selecting a checkpoint
representative of MtErie.

The methodological answer to RQ1 is therefore that experimental design changes
the scientific conclusion. The move from historical single runs to paired
replication replaces the claim that SSJ is the best variant with a more limited
and repeatable selection of standard FAM.

\subsection{RQ2: RT-DETR effectiveness and missing modalities}

On the locked MtErie benchmark, standard FAM improves Additive fusion in every
paired seed and raises mean \mapfifty{} by 0.0700. The improvement is supported
by COCO mAP, mAP@75, mAR, and the later recall analysis. IR Dropout and SSJ do
not provide a reliable mean gain over standard FAM; Identity DCNv2 is highly
variable, and Grid Sample is close but not consistently superior.

VIS+IR is better than the strongest single modality for all thirty final
RT-DETR checkpoints. Modal Dropout therefore supports a usable response when a
modality is removed without making the detector indifferent to the availability
of both signals. On the internal benchmark, multimodal information remains
valuable.

The answer is scoped to the internal development benchmark. It establishes a
consistent paired advantage for FAM over Additive under the selected protocol,
not a universal expected gain for all seeds, datasets, sensor geometries, or
implementations.

\subsection{RQ3: mechanism, levels, and bounded offsets}

The FAM predicts nonzero transformations at P3 and P4 across all five standard
checkpoints. Its offset scale and transformed features vary with seed, level,
and acquisition session. SSJ changes P3 behavior consistently, increasing
offset magnitude and smoothing, even though detection does not improve. Grid
Sample is internally more stable but does not establish that a pure warp is
equivalent to DCNv2.

The diagnostics do not demonstrate physical RGB--IR registration. DCNv2 mixes
geometric sampling, filtering, mask modulation, and channel interaction. Its
output becomes less similar to RGB at the same cell under the selected feature
proxies, while still improving detection. The supported interpretation is a
task-oriented geometric-functional transformation.

The P5 seed-41 collapse is an important counterexample to reasoning from output
accuracy alone. An internally constant branch coexists with the best FAM
checkpoint because the rest of the detector compensates for it. Factorial
ablation identifies P3 as the most stable contributor and P5 as positive but
variable; removing P5 after training is especially damaging in the collapsed
checkpoint.

Smoothly bounding offsets at four feature cells eliminates the observed
out-of-map collapse in five new runs, but lowers mean accuracy and increases
dispersion. The answer to the RQ3 sub-question is therefore asymmetric: the
intervention is mechanistically effective but is not a better detector.

\subsection{RQ4: operational relevance}

FAM primarily changes the recall side of the precision--recall trade-off. At
confidence 0.25, it improves mean recall by 0.0990 in all five paired seeds and
reduces the frequency of non-empty frames with at least one missed target. This
is relevant to SAR, where a missed person can have high cost. False positives
do not decrease consistently, however, and the 0.01 threshold used for AP
collection is not operational because it produces roughly fifty false
positives per image.

Carnation exposes a geometric boundary. FAM is more tolerant than Additive
fusion under the severe scale mismatch, but adding IR remains worse than using
VIS alone in every FAM checkpoint. FAM mitigates the damage of that mismatch;
it does not solve arbitrary scale differences between sensors.

The performance gain also has a material computational cost: 77.5\% more
parameters, 46.2\% more proxy GFLOPs, 17.9\% more detector latency, and higher
CUDA memory on the measured GPU. These values prevent describing FAM as a free
or generically real-time improvement.

\subsection{RQ5: architectural transferability}

Positive transfer is not established. Deformable DETR provides a promising
exploratory median trend, but its uncertainty intervals overlap and its
protocol is not aligned with the final campaign. Complete DINO base is too weak
under the studied configuration to justify FAM variants. Under the controlled
YOLOv10 protocol, FAM decreases mean VIS+IR performance and wins in only two of
five seeds.

The negative YOLOv10 result is scientifically useful. It shows that the RT-DETR
gain depends on the surrounding representation, fusion interface, optimization
trajectory, or checkpoint regime and cannot be assumed from the local FAM
operation alone. It does not show that YOLOv10 or DINO are intrinsically
unsuitable for RGB--thermal detection.

\section{Overall interpretation}

The most defensible conclusion is narrower than the historical narrative but
better supported. Standard FAM is the strongest studied RT-DETR configuration
because it improves Additive consistently on the internal benchmark, not
because it achieves the largest single score or because its offsets reproduce a
known camera transform. Its main observed detection benefit is higher recall.

At the same time, the module is internally variable and computationally
expensive. One feature level can collapse without visible degradation in the
headline metric, and a correction that makes the mechanism safer can make the
detector worse. Architecture transfer and external scale mismatch further show
that an alignment-inspired inductive bias is not sufficient on its own.

These results support a broader methodological point: evaluating multimodal
fusion should combine output metrics, missing-modality tests, paired
replication, internal diagnostics, out-of-distribution stress, and cost. Any one
of these views alone would have produced an incomplete model selection.

\section{Threats to validity}

\subsection{Internal validity}

Native CUDA execution is nondeterministic, particularly for deformable
operators. Pairing seeds controls intended stochastic sources but cannot pair
the microscopic CUDA perturbations of two architectures. Five independent
checkpoints estimate the resulting distribution but do not exhaust it.

The level ablations are interventions on already trained networks. Bypassed
representations are outside the training path and cannot be interpreted as the
performance of a newly trained P3-only or P3+P5 architecture. The P5 audit and
the choice of a bounded correction followed observation of the MtErie
checkpoint. Freezing one hypothesis before five new trainings reduces further
adaptation, but the correction remains post-hoc with respect to the internal
benchmark.

The standalone YOLOv10 evaluator differs slightly from the automatic
Ultralytics validator. The predefined tolerance is recorded as missed rather
than changed after inference. Comparisons among modalities use the same
standalone evaluator, while automatic VIS+IR results remain primary for the
training protocol.

\subsection{Construct validity}

\mapfifty{} emphasizes detection at IoU 0.50 and is not a complete measure of
localization quality or SAR utility. Secondary COCO mAP, mAP@75, mAR, and error
metrics reduce dependence on one score but do not measure response time,
operator workload, alarm calibration, energy consumption, or consequences of
false alarms.

Offset magnitude, feature cosine similarity, RMSE, smoothing, and PCA are
internal proxies. Without camera calibration or ground-truth correspondences,
they do not validate physical registration. PCA figures also depend on a
low-dimensional projection and are used illustratively.

\subsection{Statistical conclusion validity}

Five paired runs permit estimation of effect direction and variability but
produce wide intervals. Exact Wilcoxon tests have coarse resolution, and the
smallest attainable two-sided value for five nonzero signed pairs is 0.0625.
The reported tests are exploratory and are not corrected for the number of
contrasts. Checkpoints, rather than frames, boxes, diagnostic samples, or
feature cells, are the experimental units.

\subsection{External validity}

MtErie was repeatedly consulted during project development and is not a blind
holdout. Its final locked comparisons are internally useful but may overstate
performance on a new acquisition. The FHL validation split is too small and
shifted to support ordinary checkpoint selection for MtErie.

Carnation is one deliberately severe scale-mismatch case. It reveals a
specific boundary but cannot estimate performance across SAR domains. The
study uses one dataset, one main RT-DETR size, one YOLOv10 size, and one GPU for
latency. Different cameras, calibration, target scales, thermal conditions,
hardware, or training budgets may change the conclusions.

Deformable DETR and DINO results use the previous protocol. They cannot form a
quantitatively homogeneous architecture leaderboard with final RT-DETR and
YOLOv10.

\section{Future work}

\subsection{A representative model-selection protocol}

The first priority is not another grid on MtErie. A future study should build a
validation split exclusively from training data, separated by acquisition
session or temporal block to reduce leakage. The checkpoint rule, primary
metric, training horizon, and allowed hyperparameter search should be fixed
before the internal test or a new holdout is observed. A genuinely independent
paired acquisition would then be reserved for final confirmation.

This protocol is necessary before deciding whether the late YOLOv10
degradation is caused by overfitting, an unsuitable schedule, or validation
mismatch. It would also allow early stopping to be compared fairly across
architectures.

\subsection{Improving the alignment architecture}

The diagnostics suggest several targeted directions for a future improved
model:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
  \item normalize or condition the offset predictor on feature scale so that a
        backbone activation anomaly is less likely to generate extreme
        offsets;
  \item add a residual identity path or a learned per-level gate so that raw IR
        information remains available if the transformed branch becomes
        unreliable;
  \item regularize offset magnitude smoothly rather than applying a hard
        performance-independent bound;
  \item train level-specific variants, particularly P3-focused or gated-P5
        models, instead of inferring their behavior from post-hoc bypasses;
  \item use cross-modal consistency or modality-specific auxiliary losses to
        improve the weak branch without requiring the fusion output to dominate
        every training step;
  \item exploit calibration metadata or synthetic known transformations to
        introduce geometric supervision and distinguish registration from
        task-oriented feature transformation.
\end{enumerate}

These candidates should not all be searched on MtErie. A small number of
mechanistically justified variants should be frozen using the new validation
protocol, followed by paired replication and the same internal audit used in
this thesis.

\subsection{Broader evaluation and deployment}

Further work should include multiple independent RGB--thermal acquisitions,
controlled scale and translation perturbations, additional missing-sensor
patterns, confidence calibration, and end-to-end latency including image
loading, preprocessing, transfer, postprocessing, and visualization. Deployment
experiments should report power and memory limits of the target UAV hardware
rather than extrapolating from a laptop GPU.

\section{Conclusions}

This thesis evaluated RGB--thermal fusion for UAV Search and Rescue from a
reliability-first perspective. It began with an inherited RT-DETR Feature
Alignment Module and replaced a single-run architectural narrative with a
paired multi-seed comparison, direct inspection of learned transformations,
level interventions, external stress testing, and transfer analysis.

Standard FAM improves Additive RT-DETR on the internal MtErie benchmark in all
five paired seeds and consistently increases recall at practical sensitivity
thresholds. It remains the selected RT-DETR model. The internal evidence,
however, supports a learned geometric-functional transformation rather than
verified physical registration. The P5 collapse, the negative bounded-offset
result, the Carnation VIS-only advantage, the computational overhead, and the
negative YOLOv10 transfer collectively define where the improvement stops.

The principal outcome is therefore both architectural and methodological: FAM
is promising within RT-DETR, but a multimodal detector should not be judged by
its best checkpoint or its fusion score alone. Repeated runs, explicit
missing-modality evaluation, internal diagnostics, external stress cases, and
computational cost are necessary to determine whether an apparent fusion gain
is reliable, understandable, and transferable.
