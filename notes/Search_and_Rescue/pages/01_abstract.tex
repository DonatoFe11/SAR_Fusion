\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Unmanned Aerial Vehicles equipped with visible and thermal cameras can support
wilderness Search and Rescue by combining complementary appearance and heat
information. Their use, however, raises three coupled problems: either modality
may become unavailable or uninformative, the two sensors are not perfectly
registered, and the small size of the available data makes architectural
comparisons sensitive to training variability and model-selection choices.

This thesis studies RGB--thermal person detection on the WiSARD dataset. It
starts from an RT-DETR dual-backbone detector and a Feature Alignment Module
(FAM) developed in preceding coursework. The contribution is not the original
introduction of FAM, but its reliability-aware evaluation, mechanistic
characterization, failure-mode analysis, and transfer to other detector
families. An experimental audit revealed that preliminary single-run rankings
were not reliable. A locked protocol was therefore defined using five paired
training seeds, isolated processes, a fixed ten-epoch horizon, final
checkpoints, transferred COCO person heads, and Modal Dropout restricted to
training.

On the internal MtErie benchmark, FAM improves additive RT-DETR fusion in all
five paired seeds, increasing mean \mapfifty{} from
\(0.3080\pm0.0677\) to \(0.3780\pm0.0440\). The main behavioral effect is a
higher recall: at confidence 0.25, the mean increase is 0.0990 and is observed
in every seed. Direct inspection of offsets, masks, and feature maps confirms
that the geometric path is active at P3 and P4, but does not establish physical
RGB--IR registration. One checkpoint exhibits a compensated P5 collapse with
offsets of approximately 5,030 input pixels and a spatially constant output,
despite attaining the highest FAM detection score. A bounded-offset variant
removes the observed collapse but reduces mean detection performance, showing
that a mechanistically effective correction is not necessarily a better
detector.

Further experiments delimit the result. Under the severe scale mismatch of the
predefined Carnation stress test, FAM is more tolerant than additive fusion,
yet visible-only inference remains better than fusion in all five FAM
checkpoints. FAM also increases model size and measured detector latency. A
five-seed transfer study on a dual-backbone YOLOv10 does not reproduce the
RT-DETR gain. The thesis therefore supports FAM as the strongest studied
RT-DETR configuration on the internal benchmark, while showing that its
benefit, internal behavior, and transferability must be reported with explicit
experimental and validity boundaries.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} RGB--thermal fusion; Search and Rescue; UAV;
RT-DETR; YOLOv10; missing modalities; feature alignment; reproducibility.
