\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Unmanned Aerial Vehicles equipped with visible and thermal cameras can support
wilderness Search and Rescue by combining complementary appearance and heat
information. Their use, however, raises three main problems: either modality
may become unavailable or uninformative, the sensors are not perfectly
registered, and limited data make architectural comparisons sensitive to
training variability and checkpoint selection.

This thesis studies RGB--thermal person detection on the WiSARD dataset. It
starts from an RT-DETR dual-backbone detector and a Feature Alignment Module
(FAM) developed in preceding work. The contribution is a reliability-aware
evaluation and extension of that baseline, including paired multi-seed
comparison, mechanistic diagnostics, a complete-video validation protocol,
configuration-matched full-data confirmation, and transfer to other detector
families. Every principal campaign uses five paired seeds and a fixed
ten-epoch budget without early stopping.

In the historical locked comparison, FAM improves additive RT-DETR fusion in
all five seeds, increasing mean \mapfifty{} from
\(0.3080\pm0.0677\) to \(0.3780\pm0.0440\). Its main behavioral effect is
higher recall: at a confidence threshold of 0.25, the mean paired increase
is 0.0990 and is observed in every seed. Internal inspection of offsets, masks,
and feature maps confirms active geometric-functional transformations at P3
and P4, but does not establish physical RGB--IR registration. One high-performing
checkpoint even contains a compensated P5 collapse. A later validation-led
campaign rejects the tested addition of a P2 feature level, global \(800\times800\)
input, post-fusion reliability gates, and a scalar residual control under a
common Stage-A protocol. Reliability-Conditioned Residual Alignment (RCRA),
which locally selects how much of the FAM correction to retain, is the only
candidate to pass Stage A. In matched full-data Stage B it obtains \(0.3753\pm0.0538\),
compared with \(0.3569\pm0.0422\) for current-code FAM, but wins only three of five paired
seeds. It therefore fails the predeclared stability condition and FAM remains
the selected model.

A post-selection audit on the same 708 paired frames shows that fusion exceeds
VIS in all five selected FAM checkpoints by \(0.0373\pm0.0223\). The very low
masked-IR/VIS-ground-truth score is shown to be a missing-reference and
coordinate-contract failure rather than thermal-branch collapse. Further
experiments delimit the result: FAM mitigates but does not solve the severe
Carnation scale mismatch, increases model cost and latency, and does not yield
a controlled gain when transferred to YOLOv10. The thesis therefore supports
FAM as the strongest confirmed RT-DETR configuration in this study while
showing that apparent multimodal gains require paired replication, explicit
coordinate contracts, and predeclared model-selection rules.

\vspace{1em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} RGB--thermal fusion; Search and Rescue; UAV;
RT-DETR; YOLOv10; missing modalities; feature alignment; reproducibility.
